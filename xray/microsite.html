Where the clicks land

Paid Microsite & Landing Pages

A small, fast set of pages built for ad traffic, running beside the WordPress sites on their own codebase. Phase 1 is one audience: US employers.

The prototypes linked below are early concepts, not launch-ready pages. They exist to make the direction concrete and to give something specific to react to. Copy, imagery, claims, and forms all change before anything goes live.

  3. Short formOnly fields sales genuinely uses
  4. BookingOffer a time immediately, while intent is hot
  5. CRM recordLead lands in Zoho with campaign data attached
  6. Qualified backSales marks quality, which feeds bidding

The important part is step six. Most paid programmes stop at step five and end up optimising for whoever fills in forms most easily, rather than whoever buys.

Design rules

What makes a paid page different

Opinion
  • One audience per page. An employer page talks only to employers. Job seekers get a clear, polite route elsewhere rather than a form option that puts them into the sales pipeline.
  • One primary action. Book or enquire. Navigation that invites browsing belongs on the main site, not on a page someone was paid to reach.
  • Fast on a phone. Paid traffic is heavily mobile and impatient.
  • Short forms. Every extra field costs leads. Fields that sales does not use get removed.
  • Proof close to the action. Whatever makes VC credible sits next to the button, not three screens away.
  • Measurable by design. Instrumented as it is built, not retrofitted.

Coexistence

How this sits with the existing websites

No disruption

Paid layer

Handles ad traffic only. Separate codebase, separate deploys, own tracking. Can be changed daily without a WordPress release.

WordPress

Keeps serving organic search, brand traffic, careers, and everything else it does today. Unchanged, and owned by the existing team.

Both can point at the same brand, the same phone number, and the same CRM. Visitors never see two systems — only one clear next step.
