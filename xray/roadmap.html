In-house Performance Marketing

30 / 60 / 90 Days

What I would do in the first quarter as part of the team: prove where the opportunity is, build the system correctly, and scale only after the numbers support it. The clock starts when access and definitions are in place.

North star: cost per completed qualified demo — not cost per form fill. Planning ranges below are conservative Google Search bands for budgeting conversation only. Real economics come from Virtual Coworker’s historical data and the first weeks of live performance.

Before day one

Access, history, and definitions

Needs access

To avoid delays I would need Ads admin (Editor / API as available), GA4 and GTM, appropriate Zoho access, historical campaign and lead / completed-demo / customer data, clear definitions for qualified lead → booked demo → completed demo → closed client, and a trackable phone number if calls matter.

With that in place, the main audit, technical setup, landing pages, and initial campaign build can complete in the first week. Existing campaigns can continue in parallel. Full list on Decisions & Actions.

First month

Days 1–30 — Audit, build, launch controlled

Not started

Understand before spending

Week 1
  • Audit the existing account and historical performance Structure, conversion actions, search terms, wasted spend.
  • Identify the strongest services, geographies, keywords, and customer types Confirm with the team which US priorities come first.
  • Review how leads move through Zoho Who touches them, how fast, and where quality is marked.
  • Lock definitions for qualified lead, booked demo, completed demo, closed client One shared language for sales and marketing.

Build the paid system

Week 1–2
  • Build dedicated paid landing pages for US employers Early concepts on Landing Pages — not final.
  • Build Google Ads account structure (Editor / API workflows where useful) High-intent Search; negatives for job-seeker traffic from day one.
  • Connect lead delivery and attribution into Zoho Campaign, keyword, and landing page travel with the lead.
  • Verify tracking end to end before meaningful spend Test submissions that a human can find in CRM.

Launch focused — do not scale yet

Week 2–4
  • Launch focused Google Search campaigns for prioritized US demand
  • Begin optimizing from actual lead quality, not click volume
  • Keep the first month controlled until we know which traffic produces qualified opportunities

What good looks like: a written baseline, a working click → Zoho path, and sales recognizing the leads as the right kind of buyer.

Planning ranges

First-month Google Search bands

Not guarantees

I do not yet know Virtual Coworker’s actual cost per lead or cost per completed demo. Channel economics in the VA space vary; Google Search with dedicated landing pages often differs from social Instant Forms.

Conservative planning ranges

  • Cost per lead: ~$150–$300
  • Cost per completed qualified demo: ~$1,000–$2,000

With ~$10k–$20k first-month ad budget

  • Roughly 35–100 leads
  • Roughly 5–15 completed qualified demos

These are targets for planning, not guarantees. The main KPI remains cost per completed qualified demo.

Second month

Days 31–60 — Tighten and learn

Not started

Improve the machine

Ongoing
  • Remove weak traffic; expand successful high-intent and long-tail keywords
  • Build more landing pages around proven demand
  • Improve bidding and campaign structure
  • Compare lead quality by service, geography, and keyword
  • Begin optimizing toward completed demos
  • Increase budget only where the economics support it

What good looks like: we can say which keywords and pages produce real buyers, and spend tilts toward those.

Third month

Days 61–90 — Scale only what is proven

Not started

Grow from evidence

Week 9–12
  • Scale the campaigns producing qualified demos
  • Expand successful keyword and landing-page groups
  • Reduce or eliminate weak campaigns
  • Feed qualified outcomes back into Google Ads where possible So bidding optimizes for buyers, not form fills.
  • Establish reporting for lead cost, completed-demo cost, and customer acquisition cost
  • Build the next-stage growth plan Including whether AU employer paid is ready — see Future Expansion.

What good looks like: a month-four recommendation we would still defend in month six — made from evidence, not hope.
