How success is judged

Measurement

Three levels, in order of importance. Business outcomes decide whether the programme is working; acquisition metrics explain why; quality and operations metrics tell us what to fix next.

This is the framework, not a report. No analytics, ads, or CRM data is connected to this dashboard, so no figures appear. The first real numbers arrive after access is granted and the first campaign has run.

Business outcomes

The only level that answers "is this worth doing".

  • Completed qualified demos Paid enquiries that become a completed demo sales agrees is real Zoho
  • Cost per completed qualified demo The north-star KPI — paid spend ÷ those demos Ads + Zoho
  • Clients won from paid The number that actually pays for the programme Zoho
  • Cost per client acquired Compared with what a client is worth over time Ads + Zoho
  • Payback period How long a paid client takes to cover their acquisition cost Finance
Level 2 · explains level 1

Acquisition performance

Useful for diagnosis. Dangerous as a headline, because these can all improve while the business gets nothing.

  • Spend By campaign and by week Google Ads
  • Cost per click Watched for drift, not optimised directly Google Ads
  • Conversion rate By landing page and by ad group Ads + GA4
  • Cost per lead Raw enquiries, always shown next to cost per qualified Ads
  • Impression share lost to budget Whether we are leaving demand on the table Google Ads
  • Search terms coverage Share of spend on terms we deliberately chose Google Ads
Level 3 · tells us what to fix

Quality & operations

Cheap to track, and usually where the biggest improvements hide.

  • Lead quality rate Share of paid leads sales calls genuine Zoho
  • Wrong-audience rate Job seekers reaching an employer form Zoho + forms
  • Speed to first contact Minutes or hours, not days Zoho
  • Booking rate Enquiries that become a scheduled conversation Booking tool
  • Form abandonment Where people give up on the form Paid layer
  • Page speed on mobile Directly affects cost and conversion Paid layer
  • Tracking health Whether every conversion still fires correctly Paid layer

The one decision

Defining the ladder to a completed demo

Blocked

Everything above depends on shared definitions the team agrees once. Typical ladder:

  • Qualified lead. Sales agrees the enquiry is a real prospect.
  • Booked demo. A discovery or demo is on the calendar.
  • Completed qualified demo. The meeting happened and need was confirmed — the recommended north star for bidding and reporting.
  • Closed client. Tracked alongside for CAC and payback; often too rare for day-to-day optimization alone.

Form fills alone are too weak to steer spend. Agreement lives on Decisions & Actions. Planning ranges for CPL / demo cost are on the roadmap — targets, not guarantees, until history and live data replace them.

Guardrails

Things I will not do with the numbers

Discipline
  • No optimising to raw form fills. It reliably buys the cheapest leads, which are usually the wrong ones.
  • No counting the same lead twice. Form, call, and booking from one person is one opportunity.
  • No claiming credit for organic or brand traffic. Branded search that would have arrived anyway is reported separately.
  • No targets before a baseline. Numbers invented to sound confident become the thing everyone remembers.
  • No hiding a bad month. A programme that only reports good news cannot be trusted with the budget.

Existing tracking on the WordPress sites — the tags and IDs already in place — is documented on Tracking inventory as reference. The paid layer gets its own clean measurement rather than depending on that setup.
