Boundaries

Scope & Ownership

Who does what, written down before anything starts. The short version: I own the paid layer end to end, we share the definitions and approvals, and the existing site stays with the people who already look after it.

The existing WordPress sites are not in scope and do not need to change. They keep serving organic search, and the current team or agency keeps ownership of them. The paid layer runs beside them on its own codebase, so nothing I ship can break what already works.

Reasoning

Why a parallel layer instead of fixing the current site

Lower risk
  • Speed. A new page can ship in days. Changing a shared WordPress theme means queues, approvals, and regression risk on pages that already rank.
  • Safety. Organic traffic is an asset. The safest way to protect it is to not touch it.
  • Clean measurement. A page built for ads can be instrumented properly from day one, rather than retrofitted around existing tags.
  • Focus. An ad click needs one clear next step. A page that also serves search, careers, and general browsing is doing several jobs at once.
  • No politics. Nobody has to be told their work was wrong for this to start.
